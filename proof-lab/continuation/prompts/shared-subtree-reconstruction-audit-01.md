MODEL AND TARGET. A circuit starts with positive Boolean input wires. Each paid gate is binary NAND(u,v)=1-uv, including equal fanins; gates may share outputs. Output selection is free. There are no free constants, negations, complemented edges, or other gate types. Let f be a nonconstant total Boolean function given by its complete table on exactly d>=1 essential inputs, with N=2^d. C(f) is its minimum strict NAND gate count. The candidate algorithm either finds an optimal circuit when C(f)<=d, or correctly reports that no circuit with at most d gates exists. The proposed uniform deterministic running time is poly(d)*9^d in a normal indexed truth-table model, hence polynomial in N. Distinguish this mathematical claim from any bounded implementation.

TWO EXPLICIT SUBROUTINE LEMMAS AVAILABLE TO THIS AUDIT.

RO: Given a total Boolean table on w named variables, strict positive-leaf read-once NAND tree recognition and reconstruction takes poly(w)*6^w time. It accepts only real NAND trees with each required leaf variable once and no free negations/constants. For w=1 only the positive projection is a zero-gate tree; constants and a negative singleton are rejected. Signed-cofactor states are bookkeeping, not physical free negations. The proof uses every nonempty subtree's surjectivity, a root NAND rectangle test, and recursion on exact canonical tables of signed cofactors.

TWICE: Given a total table H(z,Y), a procedure recognizes and reconstructs a strict positive-leaf binary NAND formula whose leaves are exactly two occurrences of z and one occurrence of every variable in Y. No other leaves, constants, free NOTs, or sharing occur inside this formula. It is sound and complete, including Y empty, and uses O(poly(|Y|)*8^|Y|) time and O(5^|Y|) semantic demand states. For clarity, its proof uses states (S,k) with a syntactic leaf set S contained in Y and k in {0,1,2}; paired demands for z=0,1 are signed cofactors of the corresponding original H slice or 0,1,or wildcard. Full exact demand keys preserve syntactic S even if variables are semantically inessential. Nonempty zero rectangles uniquely determine child factors. Empty zero rectangles branch between (0,wildcard) and (wildcard,0); wildcard demands impose no restriction. Rank |S|+k strictly decreases. This subroutine's exact leaf multiset forces exactly |Y|+1 NAND gates. Do not take a generic read-twice theorem or a semantic factorization result as a substitute for this lemma. Treat the stated soundness/completeness/runtime as the interface assumption and check whether the proposed outer algorithm actually meets it.

STRUCTURAL CANDIDATE.
1. A pruned optimum circuit of s gates uses only the d essential primaries: if ambient dummy primaries were allowed, substitute a retained essential variable for each, then prune; minimality should preserve the optimum. Every nonoutput signal vertex in the ancestor DAG has positive outdegree, where each fanin occurrence is an edge, including repeated fanins. Connectedness gives s>=d-1.
2. If s=d-1, slot saturation makes the ancestor graph a read-once NAND tree, so apply RO to f first and return its d-1-gate witness if accepted.
3. If s=d, the d+s vertices and 2s fanin edges imply exactly one nonoutput signal q has outdegree two and every other nonoutput signal has outdegree one. Prove or refute that q's entire ancestral subgraph is a strict read-once NAND tree on a nonempty set W of original variables, and each variable in W occurs nowhere outside that subtree. The special case that q is a primary must be included.
4. Collapse that subtree to a new formal leaf z. The remaining object should unfold to a genuine NAND formula H(z,Y), where Y is the complement of W, z occurs exactly twice, and every Y variable occurs once. All sharing is accounted for by q. The subtree has |W|-1 gates and the outside formula |Y|+1 gates.

EXPLICIT OUTER ALGORITHM.
After RO rejects f, enumerate every nonempty subset W of the d inputs. Put Y=inputs minus W. Regard f as a matrix with rows w in {0,1}^W and columns y in {0,1}^Y. Construct each row's complete Boolean function of Y. Require exactly two distinct row types; reject this W if fewer or more. Try both labelings of these two row types as H(0,Y) and H(1,Y). The row-class map q:{0,1}^W->{0,1} is explicit. Apply RO to this q, and TWICE to the explicit two-row table H. If both accept, compose by computing q once and wiring its output to the two z leaves of H. Return the resulting d-gate DAG. If all candidates fail, reject the promise C(f)<=d.
